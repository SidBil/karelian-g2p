# Abstract

Grapheme-to-phoneme (G2P) conversion for low-resource languages typically means choosing between hand-written rule systems, which are precise but labor-intensive and brittle to lexical and phonological exceptions, and neural sequence models, which generalize better but need training data these languages rarely have. We study this trade-off on three closely related, under-documented Finnic varieties — Karelian, Livonian, and Ingrian — using orthography-IPA pairs scraped from Wiktionary (690, 2,483, and 8,241 pairs respectively). We compare a hand-written rule-based system, a character-level neural sequence-to-sequence model, and rules induced by a large language model from the same training examples, evaluating all three on phoneme error rate (PER) and word error rate (WER) alongside training and inference cost. We then test whether guiding the neural model with rule-based or LLM-induced rules — a residual-style hybrid — improves data efficiency and robustness over the neural model alone, a question motivated by our own baseline: on Karelian, the neural model needs roughly 90% of only 552 training examples to overtake a 4.43%-PER rule-based system. Finally, because the three target languages are typologically close to each other and to better-resourced Finnish, we test whether G2P knowledge transfers across them, and from Finnish, without target-language-specific rule engineering. We report the first systematic multi-method G2P comparison for these three Finnic languages and, more broadly, evidence on when rule-guidance and cross-lingual transfer can substitute for scarce training data in low-resource G2P.
